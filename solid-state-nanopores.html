01 / Solid-State Nanopore Sensing

Solid-state nanopore principles

Artificial apertures connect electrolyte solutions across a solid membrane.

With electrolyte on both sides and a potential across the membrane, ions pass through the opening. A molecule interacting with the pore can change the current. Event depth and duration provide features for investigating transport and molecular interactions.

Geometry, surface properties, solution conditions and molecular size, charge and conformation influence the response. Tunable pore architecture offers experimental flexibility, while fast transport, noise, adsorption and pore-to-pore variation can complicate interpretation. [2]

02 / Solid-State Nanopore Sensing

Molecular translocation and current signatures

A current event reflects interactions between a molecule, the pore and its environment.

An applied potential drives ionic current through the pore. Molecular entry, passage or interaction can change that current. The event depth and duration provide information about transport, but their interpretation depends on molecular properties, pore geometry, surface interactions and solution conditions.

The blockade shown here is an illustrative signal rather than a measurement. Other current responses can occur under different conditions. A generic blockade does not provide the sequence-resolution capability of ONT basecalling.

03 / Solid-State Nanopore Sensing

Applications and limitations

Solid-state nanopores support investigations of molecular transport and sensing.

Artificial pore geometries and surfaces offer flexibility for studying nucleic acids, proteins and other molecular systems. Identification may require additional recognition chemistry or carefully characterised electrical features.

Fast transport, electrical noise, nonspecific adsorption and pore-to-pore variation can limit interpretation. A sensing event alone does not establish target identity, concentration or clinical utility. [2]

05 / Solid-State Nanopore Sensing

Biological and solid-state nanopores compared

Both approaches measure ionic transport at the nanoscale. What that signal can tell us depends on the pore and the assay.

PROTEIN CHANNEL

Biological nanopores / ONT

Structure
Engineered protein pore in a membrane.
Transport & signal
A motor regulates nucleic-acid movement. Sequence context modulates current.
Identification
Basecalling and sequence matching support barcode identity. Recognition probes can connect other analytes to that identity.
Flexibility & limits
Protein engineering and probe design offer selectivity. Suitable molecular constructs, library compatibility and calibration constrain the assay.

ARTIFICIAL APERTURE

Solid-state nanopores

Structure
Nanoscale opening in an artificial solid membrane.
Transport & signal
Transport and interactions produce event features such as current change and duration.
Identification
Interpretation depends on signal features and any additional recognition chemistry. A blockade alone does not provide ONT-like sequence resolution.
Flexibility & limits
Pore geometry and surface chemistry can be adjusted. Transport control, noise and nonspecific interactions remain important challenges.

This comparison concerns ONT-style sequencing and generic solid-state ionic-current sensing. It does not imply that all biological pores sequence DNA, or that all nanopore platforms have equivalent capabilities. [1] [2]
