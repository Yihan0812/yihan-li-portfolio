PREVIOUS RESEARCH EXPERIENCE / ZHEJIANG UNIVERSITY

Solid-State Nanopore Sensing for Tear-Based Disease Fingerprinting

Exploring label-free molecular profiling and machine learning-assisted recognition of ocular disease.

During my research experience at Zhejiang University, I explored the potential of solid-state nanopore sensing for analysing tear samples from individuals with and without dry eye disease. Rather than targeting a single predefined biomarker, the research focused on the broader molecular information contained in complex biological samples.

Solid-state nanopores provide an electrical sensing interface through which molecular interactions and translocation events can generate changes in ionic current. By examining patterns across these signals, the project explored whether disease-associated differences in tear composition could be reflected in measurable electrical fingerprints.

A particular interest was the integration of nanopore signal analysis with machine learning, using collective signal characteristics to investigate the potential for distinguishing between different sample groups. This approach reflects my broader interest in combining molecular sensing with computational analysis to study complex biological systems.

The approach investigates collective signal patterns; individual molecular species were not chemically identified from these signals. This is exploratory research, not a clinically validated diagnostic method.

CENTRAL SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

Can complex nanopore signals reveal disease-associated molecular fingerprints without requiring prior identification of individual biomarkers?

This experience introduced me to the potential of combining nanoscale sensing with computational analysis, an interest that continues to inform my current research on biological nanopore-based molecular detection and quantification.

Tear proteomics and mass-spectrometry metabolomics support the motivation for broad molecular profiling. They use different sensing and identification methods from nanopore ionic-current fingerprinting.
