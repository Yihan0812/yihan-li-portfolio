# Computational and Experimental Investigation of White Phosphorus Activation

I investigated how interactions between white phosphorus, organic disulfides and bases might initiate phosphorus–sulfur bond formation and P–P cleavage. The project combined mechanistic hypotheses, Gaussian-based structural exploration and experimental investigation without establishing a definitive mechanism.

**Central question:** How can base-mediated interactions promote the transformation of white phosphorus into phosphorus–sulfur-containing compounds, and what intermediate structures and P–P cleavage pathways might be involved?

## Alternative activation hypotheses

The notebook explores Et3N and KHMDS separately. Candidate sulfur activation, N–S-containing species, thiolate chemistry and direct interactions with phosphorus-containing compounds were considered. These ideas generated alternative P4-opening, ring, pendant and fragmentation sketches rather than a single verified pathway.

## Computational exploration of reaction pathways

Gaussian was used to investigate candidate intermediates and possible transition-state structures. Several optimisation attempts did not retain expected or chemically plausible bonding arrangements. The surviving notes contain exploratory energy annotations but not the output files or validation needed for a quantitative energy profile.

## Experimental investigation of mechanistic hypotheses

NMR reaction monitoring and controls were used alongside trapping strategies and GC–MS. The photographed record documents mixed-disulfide comparisons and a five-peak GC–MS note with tentative sulfur-species assignments. The notebook later recognises that background disulfide exchange limits the diagnostic value of detecting a mixed disulfide. A reported N–S assignment does not establish an isolated or uniquely identified intermediate.

## Research reflection

The work linked mechanistic proposal, structural calculation and experimental reassessment. It left both the detailed activation sequence and the identities of phosphorus-containing intermediates unresolved, while clarifying which observations would require more discriminating controls and structural evidence.
