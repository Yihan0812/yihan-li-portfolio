RESEARCH / IMPERIAL COLLEGE LONDON

Medicinal Chemistry & Drug Discovery

My research interests lie in understanding how molecular structure and chemical interactions influence biological activity, and how these relationships can guide the design of potential therapeutic compounds. Through computational molecular modelling and experimental organic synthesis, I have explored different approaches to rational drug design and molecular optimisation.

01 / COMPUTATIONAL MOLECULAR DESIGN

Structure-Based Design of SARS-CoV-2 Main Protease Inhibitors

This project explored the structure-guided design of non-covalent SARS-CoV-2 main protease inhibitors. Molecular docking and protein–ligand interaction analysis were used to evaluate candidate scaffolds and guide the design of three new analogues. A synthetic route and enzyme inhibition assay were subsequently proposed to support future experimental evaluation.

Computational modelling completed; synthesis and biological evaluation proposed.

02 / EXPERIMENTAL MEDICINAL CHEMISTRY

SAR-Guided Design and Synthesis of Antileishmanial Acyl Urea Analogues

Exploring structure–activity relationships in an antileishmanial acyl urea series, this project investigated how molecular modifications could influence physicochemical properties and potential biological activity. Two designed analogues were synthesised through a two-step reaction sequence and characterised using spectroscopic and analytical techniques.

F1 and meta-chloro F2 synthesised; biological activity untested.
