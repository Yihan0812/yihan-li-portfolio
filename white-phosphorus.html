RESEARCH 03 / PHOSPHORUS ACTIVATION

Computational and Experimental Investigation of White Phosphorus Activation

I investigated how interactions between white phosphorus, organic disulfides and bases might initiate phosphorus–sulfur bond formation and P–P cleavage. The project combined mechanistic hypotheses, Gaussian-based structural exploration and experimental investigation without establishing a definitive mechanism.

Reconstructed from nine notebook photographs, in the supplied order, with uncertainties retained.

Overview Mechanisms Computational Experimental Research Reflection

A molecular-level mechanistic question

How can base-mediated interactions promote the transformation of white phosphorus into phosphorus–sulfur-containing compounds, and what intermediate structures and P–P cleavage pathways might be involved?

Published disulfide-mediated functionalisation supplies the context. The notebook explores different possible roles for organic and inorganic bases and several ways of opening or fragmenting the phosphorus framework.

Computational Exploration of Reaction Pathways

Gaussian was used to investigate candidate phosphorus-containing intermediates, alternative P–P cleavage pathways and possible transition-state structures. Several optimisation attempts did not give the expected connectivity or chemically plausible geometry. This motivated reassessment of structural hypotheses, without proving every unsuccessful candidate chemically impossible.

Intermediate-trapping strategies

Mixed aryl disulfides were considered as distinguishable probes of sulfur-species exchange. Bulky/Lewis-pair approaches to P₃ trapping were also proposed, but the exact trapping reagent and a successfully isolated P₃ species are not established.

Research Reflection

The work developed through mechanistic proposal, computational exploration, experimental comparison and reassessment. This is a conceptual research cycle, not an invented chronological log. The surviving notes show how apparently useful evidence—such as mixed-disulfide formation—can require a more discriminating control before supporting a mechanistic claim.

The detailed activation sequence and the identities of phosphorus-containing intermediates remained unresolved. The page therefore presents the questions, candidate structures and evidence limits that shaped the investigation.
